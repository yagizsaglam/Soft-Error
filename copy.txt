■ 图片2 — 文件：C1d3
短标题：Workflow of Process
短说明：Design of the feedback control loop for scanning and printing systems
小节图片：
    C1d2-1（右大）：Each oyster print was 3D scanned, producing a digital counterpart through which its material and geometric characteristics could be exact copy. The translation from physical object to digital model introduced small errors and approximations introduced by the scanning technology and by each material's ability to be digitally captured. To reproduce the object, this imperfect digital model must then become the source for a new print, a physicalisation of an already translated version of the original. Further deviations emerge as reflecting the information contained within the scan. The resulting sequence, physical object, digital scan, physical reproduction, therefore produces iterations rather than identical copies. This imperfect doubling reflects a broader condition of architectural production, where digital models operate as imperfect projections of physical instruction. The following analyses probe this digital to physical to digital of a new methodology.
    C1d2-2（右大）：The process begins with 3D scanning oyster shells, translating complex organic geometries into point cloud data. Variations in lighting, resolution, and capture method (photogrammetry vs LiDAR) introduce scan error, producing noise and inconsistencies in the digital model. This data is converted into a mesh and optimised for fabrication, introducing further approximation. The model is then translated into layered toolpaths for 3D printing across multiple materials, where machine tolerances and material behaviour generate print error. This scan-to-print cycle is repeated across successive generations, allowing errors to accumulate with resulting outputs revealing a chain of approximations.

■ 图片5 — 文件：C1d6
短标题：Computer Simulation
短说明：Iterative Simulation in Houdini Based on Feedback-Loop Data
正文（点击该图后，在 3D 模型面板显示）：
Calculating the deformation factor begins with measuring local geometric deviation, defined as the magnitude of the displacement vector between the reference mesh and the target mesh at each point. These local deviations are then aggregated to produce a global value, representing the average displacement across the entire mesh.

To visualise this dynamically, the VEX code applies the calculated deformation factor iteratively to each point along its normal vector. The system is further refined to compute distinct error vectors between models, differentiating between scan error and print error. These are accumulated across successive generations, producing a continuous cycle of distortion and approximation.

Geometric error is reinterpreted as a spatial and generative condition rather than a static imperfection. By translating error into spatial configurations with architectural qualities, it becomes an active driver of form, capable of producing differentiated typologies and informing new modes of design.
小节图片：
    C1d5-1（右大）：The diagram outlines the simulation methodology developed to model and predict the occurring geometric errors across the full scan-print cycle. The process begins with the datafication of a single iteration, tracing the transformation from the original shell through 3D scanning to physical printing, and uses this as the basis for error quantification.
At the core of the diagram is an “Approximation Error Analysis” that identifies two distinct layers of deviation. The first layer captures errors inherent to the scanning process itself: scanner resolution limits, operator handling inconsistencies, and surface noise. The second layer addresses errors introduced during printing: material accumulation, shrinkage, and printer tolerance. Each layer produces a geometric error vector, v@error_scan_vector and v@error_print_vector, which serve as the numerical inputs to the simulation.
These vectors are fed into a Houdini Simulation Engine Solver, which sequentially applies scan distortion and print distortion to an input geometry, generating a dynamic error visualisation before producing an output. Critically, the solver operates within a feedback loop, allowing the distorted output of one iteration to inform the next, compounding errors across successive generations in the same way they accumulate in repeated physical scan-print cycles.
The simulative output, the geometry resulting after N iterations, is then passed into an analysis stage. This final phase serves three purposes: predicting how material behaviour and form will shift across successive generations, identifying structural weak points and process constraints, and mapping potential future directions for the research.
Rather than treating approximation error as a problem to be corrected, this diagram reflects instrumentalization of indeterminacy. By encoding the stochastic behaviour of the scan-print cycle into a computational framework, the work moves beyond the documentation of deviation toward its active integration, positioning approximation error as a generative agent within the design process.

■ 图片6 — 文件：C1d7
短标题：Scanned Point Cloud Model
短说明：Discrete scan points define and approximated field of geometry
小节图片：
    C1d6-1（右大）：Point cloud models describe geometry as a field of discrete coordinates, where each point carries a positional value but also implies a spatial tolerance or radius of occupation. This means that rather than defining a continuous surface, the scan produces a distributed approximation of space, dependent on resolution, density, and capture conditions.
    C1d6-2（左小）：When converted into a mesh, these points are interpolated into continuous surfaces through triangulation; the mesh model is not a direct representation of the physical object, but a constructed interpretation based on approximated spatial relationships. This introduces a disconnect in the physical-to-digital translation of geometry, exposing the limitations of digital precision.
    C1d6-3（中小）：the interaction of human intention, digital instruction, and material indeterminacy. Rather than assigning a fixed geometry to the point, these agents negotiate its volume through printing, producing unique material interactions that collectively transform digital information into physical form. 
    C1d6-4（右大）：Traditional 3D printing translates a predefined mesh into physical form through sequential as instructions for volumetric deposition. Rather than reproducing a continuous surface, the physical object emerges through the collective materialization of discrete points.

Extrusion material: Resin 
We tested resins with varying work and cure times (5 min, 10 min, and 3 hour work times ) within a perlite substrate to evaluate control over extrusion and resulting typologies. The slowest-setting resin proved inefficient, while the fastest set too quickly, risking nozzle blockage and limiting workable time. The medium-setting resin offered the most effective balance between work ability and controlled extrusion. 
    C2d3-4（右大）：Resin was tested across varied substrates to evaluate how aggregate density and material properties affect its behaviour and resulting morphology. Recycled glass, due to its high density, restricted flow, making resin extrusion more difficult even under pressure. In contrast, the gel substrate caused the resin to reverse direction after extrusion, flowing upward and forming elongated, funnel-like geometries. Dense sand similarly limited extrusion, as closely packed grains reduced available void space. Sawdust, with its low density and porous structure, allowed greater dispersion, enabling the resin to move more freely within the substrate bed.
